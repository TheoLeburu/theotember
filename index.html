Theotember

noun/ˌθiː.oʊˈtɛm.bər/  ·  thee-oh-TEM-ber

  1. September, when it belongs to someone named Theo; the whole month treated as one long birthday rather than a single day. Don't put anything in my calendar for Theotember.
  2. The turn of the year in your favour — the stretch where winter finally lets go and things start growing again. The jacarandas came out early this Theotember.
  3. informal. A cheerfully admitted excuse to celebrate for thirty days straight. It's not a whole month of cake. It's Theotember.
  4. by extension. Any stretch of time a person claims outright as their own, birthday or not. She takes a Theotember every year and comes back better for it.

Pronunciation

Four syllables, stress on the third: /ˌθiː.oʊˈtɛm.bər/ — thee-oh-TEM-ber.

The first syllable is thee, as in Theodore, not thay. The -tember is carried over from September untouched, which is what lets the blend survive being said out loud: the ear finds the month before the sentence has finished.

Origin

A blend of Theo — short for Theodore, from the Greek theós ("god") and dōron ("gift") — and September, from the Latin septem ("seven"), the seventh month of the old Roman calendar before Julius and Augustus pushed it back two places.

Formed on the pattern of Movember and Decembeard, where a month is renamed after the thing it gets given over to.

The pattern is productive but fussier than it looks. The month supplies the ending, the subject supplies the front, and the join has to survive being spoken. Theo and September overlap on nothing and lose nothing, so both halves arrive intact — which is more than most coinages of this shape manage.

First recorded use

2026, Gaborone, Botswana.

Derived forms

Theotemberish, adjective. Unhurried and faintly celebratory; done at the pace of someone with a whole month in hand. A Theotemberish attitude to a deadline.

to Theotember, verb. To stretch a single occasion across a month. He Theotembered his fortieth and regrets nothing.

Notes on use

Takes no article: you say in Theotember, not in the Theotember. Capitalised like any other month.

It does not replace September. The calendar still says September; Theotember is September claimed. Both words point at the same thirty days and disagree only about whose they are.

Botswana keeps a lot in September — the jacarandas, the end of the cold, Independence Day on the 30th — so sense 2 carries more weight here than it would elsewhere.

Coined by Theo Leburu, who was born in September and saw no reason to keep it to one day.
