Theotember

noun/ˌθiː.oʊˈtɛm.bər/  ·  thee-oh-TEM-ber

  1. September, when it belongs to someone named Theo; the whole month treated as one long birthday rather than a single day. Don't put anything in my calendar for Theotember.
  2. The turn of the year in your favour — the stretch where winter finally lets go and things start growing again. The jacarandas came out early this Theotember.
  3. informal. A cheerfully admitted excuse to celebrate for thirty days straight. It's not a whole month of cake. It's Theotember.

Origin

A blend of Theo — short for Theodore, from the Greek theós ("god") and dōron ("gift") — and September, from the Latin septem ("seven"), the seventh month of the old Roman calendar before Julius and Augustus pushed it back two places.

Formed on the pattern of Movember and Decembeard, where a month is renamed after the thing it gets given over to.

First recorded use

2026, Gaborone, Botswana.

Notes on use

Takes no article: you say in Theotember, not in the Theotember. Capitalised like any other month.

Botswana keeps a lot in September — the jacarandas, the end of the cold, Independence Day on the 30th — so sense 2 carries more weight here than it would elsewhere.

Coined by Theo Leburu, who was born in September and saw no reason to keep it to one day.
